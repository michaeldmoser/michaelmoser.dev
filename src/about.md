## How I work

Most of the problems I find interesting aren't purely technical. Something can be technically correct and still be the wrong call if nobody can realistically support it once it's part of the normal workflow. So a lot of my time goes to the less glamorous questions:

- Is this the root cause, or just the symptom we happened to notice first?
- What does this look like in six months, once other teams start depending on it?
- Who ends up supporting it, and will they understand what they're looking at?

I'd rather be honest about what we don't know than sound certain. "We don't know yet" is a perfectly good answer, as long as it comes with a plan for finding out. When something is on fire I try to be the calm one in the room, and when I'm giving feedback I try to be direct without making it about anyone's ego.

## What I care about

- Software that can be maintained by the people who actually have to maintain it, not just the person who wrote it.
- Tradeoffs said out loud. Most decisions are a choice between two imperfect options, and it helps to say which imperfections we picked (and why).
- Explaining why something matters, not just what happened.
